item 1 Random variables, expectation, variance & covariance

ConceptFormula / one-liner
Random variableA function X: outcomes → numbers
CDFF(x) = P(X ≤ x); P(a < X ≤ b) = F(b) − F(a)
ExpectationE[X] = Σ x p(x) = ∫ x f(x) dx; linear always: E[aX+bY+c] = aE[X] + bE[Y] + c
Indicator trickE[1_A] = P(A); expected count = sum of probabilities
VarianceE[X²] − (E X)²; Var(aX+b) = a²Var(X)
CovarianceE[XY] − E[X]E[Y]; Corr = Cov/(σₓσ_y) ∈ [−1,1] (linear only)
Var of a sumVar X + Var Y + 2Cov(X,Y); wᵀΣw in general
Independent ⇒ ?Cov = 0, Var(X±Y) = VarX + VarY; but Cov = 0 ⇏ independent
Total expectation / varianceE[Y] = E[E[Y|X]]; Var Y = E[Var(Y|X)] + Var(E[Y|X])
Standard error of the meanσ/√n
Unbiased variances² with divisor n − 1 (s itself still biased)
Chebyshev / MarkovP(|X−μ| ≥ kσ) ≤ 1/k²; P(X ≥ t) ≤ E[X]/t
Jensenconvex g: E[g(X)] ≥ g(E[X])
Mahalanobisd² = (x−μ)ᵀΣ⁻¹(x−μ) ~ χ²ₚ for normal data
Correlated Bernoulli sumVar(D) = nPD(1−PD)[1 + (n−1)ρ]
CLT(X̄−μ)/(σ/√n) → N(0,1); slow for skewed data and rare events

item 2 Core distributions and how they relate

DistributionOne line to remember
Binomial(n,p)Successes in n independent trials; mean np, var np(1−p); normal-ish if np and n(1−p) ≥ 10
Poisson(λ)Rare events in a window; mean = var = λ; limit of Binomial; sums of Poissons are Poisson
Exponential(λ)Gap between Poisson events; memoryless; constant hazard; mean 1/λ
Gamma(a, β)Sum of a exponentials; mean a/β; CV = 1/√a; χ²(k) = Gamma(k/2, ½)
NormalSum of many small effects; closed under linear combinations; 95% within 1.96σ
Lognormalexp of a normal; product of many factors; mean e^(μ+σ²/2) > median e^μ
χ²(k)Σ Z²; mean k, var 2k; (n−1)s²/σ² ~ χ²(n−1)
t(ν)Z/√(χ²/ν); fat tails; moments exist only below ν; → Normal as ν → ∞
F(d₁,d₂)Ratio of scaled χ²s; compares variances; t² = F(1,ν)
Beta(a,b)Distribution on [0,1]; posterior Beta(a+k, b+n−k); pseudo-counts

item 3 Conditional probability and Bayes’ theorem

IdeaOne line
Conditional probabilityP(A|B) = P(A∩B)/P(B): restrict to B, renormalise
Chain ruleP(A∩B∩C) = P(A)·P(B|A)·P(C|A∩B)
Total probabilityP(B) = Σ P(B|Aᵢ)P(Aᵢ) over a partition
IndependenceP(A∩B) = P(A)P(B); lift = 1
BayesP(A|B) = P(B|A)P(A)/P(B)
Odds formposterior odds = prior odds × LR; LR⁺ = sens/FPR; LR⁻ = (1−sens)/(1−FPR)
PrecisionπTPR / (πTPR + (1−π)FPR): driven by prevalence π as much as by the model
Decision thresholdAct when P(event | data) > c_FP / (c_FP + c_FN)
Beta–BinomialBeta(a,b) + k of n → Beta(a+k, b+n−k)
Gamma–PoissonGamma(a,b) + k events in n periods → Gamma(a+k, b+n)
Normal–NormalPrecisions add; posterior mean is precision-weighted
Shrinkage(a+k)/(a+b+n): small segments pulled toward the book mean
Prosecutor's fallacyP(match|innocent) ≠ P(innocent|match); expect (N−1)/X other matches
Credible vs confidence intervalCredible: probability statement about θ. Confidence: statement about the procedure.

item 4 Law of large numbers and the central limit theorem

IdeaOne line
LLNX̄ₙ → μ if E|X| < ∞ (weak: in probability; strong: almost surely)
Gambler's fallacyNo compensation: surplus stays ~√n, proportion converges by dilution
ChebyshevP(|X̄ − μ| ≥ ε) ≤ σ²/(nε²): always true, usually loose
Standard errorσ/√n: 4× data halves it; 100× data gains a digit
Required nn ≈ (1.96σ/m)² for margin m
CLT(X̄ − μ)/(σ/√n) → N(0,1); needs finite variance
Speedskew(X̄) = skew(X)/√n; Berry–Esseen error ≤ 0.4748·E|X−μ|³/(σ³√n)
Heavy tailsα ≤ 1: no LLN; 1 < α ≤ 2: LLN but no √n CLT; Cauchy mean is Cauchy
Dependencen_eff = n/[1 + 2Σρₖ]; AR(1): n(1−ρ)/(1+ρ)
Binomial → normalN(np, npq) with continuity correction k ± ½; use Poisson for rare events
Delta methodSE[g(X̄)] ≈ |g′(μ)|σ/√n; breaks when g′(μ) = 0
SlutskyReplace σ by s and the CLT still holds (s → σ)
Rare proportionsPrecision is set by events: ±10% needs about 384(1−p) events; prefer Wilson
Correlated portfolioLoss-rate spread does not vanish as N grows; independent-normal understates tails

IdeaOne line
Sampling distributionDistribution of a statistic over repeated samples; you see one draw
SD vs SESD: spread of data (σ). SE: spread of an estimate (σ/√n for a mean)
FPC× √((N−n)/(N−1)) when sampling without replacement from a finite population
Common SEs√(pq/n); √(σ₁²/n₁+σ₂²/n₂); median 1.2533σ/√n; (1−ρ²)/√n
MSEBias² + Variance
Variance divisorsn−1 unbiased; n+1 minimises MSE for normal data

UMVUBest unbiased estimator uses the sufficient statistic (German tank: (1+1/n)max − 1)
EfficiencyRelative to the data: mean for normal, median/trimmed for heavy tails; CRLB gives the floor
Shrinkagec* = μ²/(μ² + σ²/n): trade a little bias for a lot of variance
Normal samplingX̄ ⟂ s; (n−1)s²/σ² ~ χ²ₙ₋₁; t = (X̄−μ)/(s/√n) ~ tₙ₋₁
BootstrapSD of statistic over resamples with replacement; jackknife leaves one out
Design effectDEFF = 1 + (m−1)ρ; n_eff = rows/DEFF; use cluster-robust SEs

item 6 Maximum likelihood estimation

IdeaOne line
LikelihoodL(θ) = P(data | θ), as a function of θ; not a distribution over θ
Score and MLES(θ) = ℓ′(θ); the MLE solves S(θ̂) = 0
Fisher informationI(θ) = −E[ℓ″] per observation; SE ≈ 1/√(nI(θ̂))
Closed-form MLEsBernoulli k/n; Poisson x̄; exponential 1/x̄; normal μ = x̄, σ² = SS/n
InvarianceMLE of g(θ) is g(θ̂)
Large-n propertiesConsistent, asymptotically normal and efficient; small-sample bias O(1/n)
Wald / LR / scoreLR and score are better near boundaries and are invariant (Wald is not)
Wilks2[ℓ(θ̂) − ℓ(θ̂₀)] → χ²_q; 3.84 for one restriction at 5%
Newtonθ ← θ − S/ℓ″; needs a good start; may find local maxima or leave the parameter space
Logistic regressionConcave log-likelihood, fitted by Newton/IRLS; Cov = (X′WX)⁻¹ with W = p(1−p)
SeparationMLE at infinity; use penalty (ridge/MAP) or Firth
SandwichA⁻¹BA⁻¹ gives valid SEs under misspecification; Poisson with overdispersion φ has SE too small by √φ
CensoringEvents contribute f(t), censored contribute S(c); rate = events/exposure
AIC−2ℓ̂ + 2k: likelihood penalised for the number of parameters

item 7 Confidence intervals

IdeaOne line
MeaningThe procedure covers the truth in 95% of repeated samples; this one interval either does or does not
Meanx̄ ± t_{n−1}·s/√n; using z with estimated σ undercovers at small n
DualityThe 95% interval = all θ₀ not rejected by the 5% test
Planningn = (zσ/m)²; proportion z²p(1−p)/m²; halve margin ⇒ 4× n
ProportionPrefer Wilson, Jeffreys or likelihood-ratio; Clopper–Pearson if a guarantee is needed; avoid Wald
Zero eventsRule of three: upper 95% bound ≈ 3/n
Poisson / correlation / SDGarwood (χ²); Fisher z (atanh, SE 1/√(n−3)); χ² interval for σ
Profile likelihoodDrop in the profile log-likelihood ≤ 1.92; asymmetric, invariant
BootstrapPercentile, basic, normal; BCa or studentised for accuracy; still approximate at small n
Comparing groupsInterval for the difference, not overlap of intervals (equal SE: significant beyond 2.77 SE, overlap until 3.92 SE)
Many intervalsP(all cover) = 0.95ᵐ; Bonferroni uses level 1 − α/m
CI vs PICI width → 0 as n → ∞; PI width → ±1.96σ

item 8 Bootstrap and resampling

IdeaOne line
BootstrapResample n points with replacement from the data; recompute the statistic B times; its spread estimates the sampling spread
CentreThe bootstrap distribution is centred at θ̂, not θ: it estimates spread and shape, not location
SE / biasSE = SD(θ*); bias = mean(θ*) − θ̂; bias-correct only when bias ≳ 0.25 SE
JackknifeLeave-one-out; fine for smooth statistics, fails for the median
FailuresMaxima, infinite-variance tails, boundaries, tiny n, dependent data; use m-out-of-n, subsampling or a parametric bootstrap
IntervalsBCa or bootstrap-t for second-order accuracy; percentile/basic/normal are first-order
RegressionPairs or wild bootstrap under heteroskedasticity; residual bootstrap needs constant variance
Permutation testShuffle labels under H₀; p = (1 + #extreme)/(1 + B); exact for any statistic if labels are exchangeable
Unequal variancesRaw permutation of the mean difference is invalid for equal-means H₀; studentise or use a bootstrap test that imposes H₀
Monte Carlo pSE ≈ √(p(1−p)/B); use B ≥ 999–9,999; never report p = 0
Time seriesMoving block bootstrap, ℓ ≈ n^{1/3}; iid bootstrap understates SE by ≈ √((1+φ)/(1−φ))
ClustersResample whole clusters; design effect 1 + (k − 1)ρ
Model metricsStratified bootstrap for one AUC; paired (same cases) bootstrap for AUC differences

item 9 P-values from scratch to expert

ConceptOne line
p-valueP(statistic at least this extreme | H₀ and all model assumptions true)
Under H₀p ~ Uniform(0,1) (continuous statistics); P(p ≤ α) = α
Two-sided2 × min(tail); decide direction before seeing data
CI duality95% CI excludes null value ⇔ two-sided p < 0.05
z ≈ effect / SELarge n shrinks SE → tiny effects become significant
Power1 − β; ∝ effect·√n/σ; n ≈ 15.7/d² per group for 80% / α=.05
m testsP(≥1 false positive) = 1 − (1−α)m; Bonferroni α/m; Holm; BH for FDR
False-positive riskα(1−π) / [α(1−π) + power·π]
Min Bayes factorBF₀₁ ≥ −e·p·ln p; p = .05 → ≥ 0.41
Surprisals = −log₂ p bits (p=.05 → 4.3 bits ≈ 4 heads in a row)
Permutation p(count + 1) / (B + 1)
EquivalenceTOST: both one-sided tests reject ⇔ 90% CI inside ±Δ
Fisher combine−2 Σ ln pᵢ ~ χ²2k
Neversay “probability H₀ is true”, “accept H₀”, or report only “p < 0.05”

items 9–10 Hypothesis testing, power and experimental design

IdeaOne line
Errorsα = P(reject | H₀); power = P(reject | H₁) = 1 − β
Noncentralityλ = effect / SE; power is the area of N(λ,1) beyond the critical value
MDE≈ 2.8 SE for 80% power at two-sided α = 0.05 (z₁₋α/₂ + z_power)
Sample sizen = 2(z + z)²/d² per group; halve the effect ⇒ 4× n; 90% instead of 80% power ⇒ ×1.34
Effect sizesReport d, risk difference, OR/RR, NNT, AUC = Φ(d/√2); always with an interval
Observed powerA function of p; p = 0.05 ⇒ ~50%; adds nothing. Use the CI and pre-specified MDE instead
Type S / MLow power + significance filter ⇒ exaggerated, occasionally wrong-signed estimates
CUPEDVariance × (1 − ρ²); covariate must be pre-treatment
Allocationn₁:n₂ = (σ₁/σ₂)·√(c₂/c₁); 50/50 for equal variance and cost
ClustersDE = 1 + (m − 1)ρ; power limited by number of clusters; plateau as m grows
SequentialPeeking inflates α (5 looks ≈ 14%); Pocock = constant bound, O’Brien–Fleming = strict early, ~1.98 at the end

item 11 Linear regression done properly

IdeaOne line
OLSβ̂ = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy; slope = Cov(x,y)/Var(x); residuals ⟂ columns of X
FWLA coefficient is the slope of residualised y on residualised x
Omitted variableBias of the short regression = β₂ · δ; control for confounders, not mediators or colliders
PrecisionSE(β̂₁) = σ/(s_x√(n−1)): more n, less noise, more spread in x
AssumptionsLinearity/exogeneity ⇒ unbiased; constant variance and independence ⇒ correct SEs; normality ⇒ exact small-n tests
R²Rises with every added predictor; use adjusted R² or out-of-sample R²
IntervalsCI for the mean: √h₀; PI for a new point: √(1 + h₀); both widen away from x̄
DiagnosticsResiduals vs fitted (curve, funnel), Q–Q (tails), leverage and Cook’s D (influence)
InfluenceNeeds high leverage and a sizeable residual; Cook’s D > 4/n looks, > 1 alarms
Robust SEHC3 for small n; WLS if variance is modelled; cluster-robust for grouped data
Measurement errorSlope on a noisy x shrinks by the reliability λ
Regression to the meanUnits selected for an extreme first value regress toward the mean by (1 − ρ) with no intervention
Log modelslog y ⇒ ≈ 100β % per unit; exp(prediction) is the median: multiply by the smearing factor for means

item 12 Multicollinearity, interactions and nonlinearity

IdeaOne line
CollinearityInflates variance by 1/(1 − R²ⱼ) (VIF), not bias; leaves predictions in-sample and joint effects intact
VIFRegress xⱼ on the others; VIF = 1/(1 − R²); √VIF scales the SE; 10 is the old alarm level
Perfect collinearityXᵀX singular; coefficients unidentified (dummy trap)
RemediesJoint F-test, combine variables, more data or design, ridge/PCA for prediction; no stepwise deletion
InteractionEffect of x depends on d: β₁ is the slope at d = 0, β₃ the slope difference
CentringMakes main effects mean “at the average”; same fit, better-behaved coefficients
Marginal effectβ₁ + β₃z with Var = V₁₁ + z²V₃₃ + 2zV₁₃; plot it with a band
Interaction powerSE ≈ 2× that of a main effect; need ≈ 4× the sample for the same effect size
PolynomialsGlobal, wild at the edges and outside the data; rarely above degree 3
SplinesLocal pieces joined at knots; hinge (linear) or truncated cubic basis; knots are the tuning parameter
Cross-validationChoose flexibility by held-out error; one-SE rule favours the smoother model
Partial residualse + β̂ⱼxⱼ against xⱼ shows the shape of the effect of xⱼ
BinningMedian split loses ≈ 36% of the information; prefer splines or the raw variable

item 13 Generalised linear models

IdeaOne line
GLMy ~ exponential family, g(E[y]) = Xβ; fit by IRLS (Newton for canonical links)
Logitlog(p/(1−p)) = Xβ; e^β = odds ratio (constant); Δp ≈ β p(1−p) (varies)
OR vs RROR ≈ RR only when the event is rare (< ~10%)
TestsWald (SE), LR (two fits, best), score (null fit only); all χ²₁
SeparationPerfect split ⇒ β → ∞; use Firth or a penalty; MLE is biased outward in small samples
MetricsAUC (rank), precision depends on base rate, calibration is separate from discrimination
Poissonlog link, e^β = rate ratio, offset log(exposure) with coefficient 1
Overdispersionφ̂ = Pearson/df > 1: quasi-Poisson, sandwich or negative binomial (Var = μ + αμ²)
DevianceD = 2(ℓ_sat − ℓ̂); drop in D between nested models ~ χ²(df); AIC = −2ℓ + 2p
Pseudo-R²McFadden 1 − ℓ̂/ℓ̂₀; 0.2–0.4 is already very good
LinksLogit ≈ probit (÷1.7); cloglog is asymmetric; they differ in the tails
UndersamplingSlopes unchanged, intercept pushed up by −log r; add log r back before using probabilities

item 14 Regularisation: ridge, lasso and elastic net

IdeaOne line
Why penaliseOLS variance ≈ σ²p/n; a little bias buys a large variance reduction
Ridgeβ̂ = (XᵀX + λI)⁻¹Xᵀy; shrinks along PCs by d²/(d²+λ); never exactly zero; df = Σd²/(d²+λ)
StandardiseAlways scale predictors before penalising; leave the intercept unpenalised; do it inside CV folds
LassoL1 penalty ⇒ exact zeros; orthonormal case = soft-threshold sign(z)(|z|−λ)₊
AlgorithmCoordinate descent with warm starts along a decreasing λ path; λ_max = max|xⱼᵀy|/n
Elastic netα‖β‖₁ + (1−α)½‖β‖²; grouping effect for correlated predictors; can select > n
Choosing λK-fold CV: λ_min (best) or λ_1se (sparser); λ itself is noisy
InferenceCoefficients are biased; post-selection p-values and CIs are invalid; use splitting or debiasing
Penalised GLMsPenalised IRLS; cures separation; choose λ by CV log-loss or deviance
Adaptive lassoWeights 1/|β̂|^γ give the oracle property; lasso alone over-selects
Stability selectionSelection frequency across half-samples; keep variables above ~60–80%
Bayesian viewRidge = Gaussian prior, lasso = Laplace prior, penalised estimate = posterior mode

item 15 Model selection

IdeaOne line
Error decompositionE[error] = σ² + bias² + variance; U-shaped in flexibility
OptimismTest error ≈ training error + 2kσ²/n (Cp); training error always prefers the biggest model
AIC−2ℓ + 2k; aims at prediction (efficient); AICc for small n/k
BIC−2ℓ + k log n; approximates marginal likelihood; consistent; prefers smaller models
Akaike weightsw ∝ exp(−Δ/2); Δ < 2 means essentially tied; average predictions across models
K-fold CVK = 5–10; LOO nearly unbiased but high variance; LOO shortcut e/(1 − h) for linear models
LeakageRepeat every data-dependent step (screening, scaling, imputation) inside each fold
Winner’s curseBest-of-M CV score is optimistic; use a test set or nested CV for the whole procedure
StepwiseInvalid p-values, inflated R², unstable; prefer shrinkage or pre-specification
Time-ordered dataOut-of-time and rolling-origin validation; random CV leaks neighbours
MetricsAUC for ranking; log-loss/Brier for probabilities; match the decision
InstabilityBagging, model averaging or shrinkage reduce the variance of selection

item 16 Calibration and proper scoring rules

IdeaOne line
CalibrationP(Y = 1 | forecast q) = q; separate from discrimination (AUC)
Reliability diagramBinned mean forecast vs observed rate; below the diagonal = over-prediction; flat curve = over-confident
Proper ruleExpected loss minimised by reporting the true p; Brier, log-loss, spherical are strictly proper; accuracy and |q − y| are not
Brier decompositionBS = REL − RES + UNC; BSS = 1 − BS/UNC
Calibration slope / interceptlogit P(Y) = α + β logit q; ideal 0 and 1; β < 1 over-fit, β > 1 under-confident
ECEBin-weighted |f − o|; depends on bins and is upward-biased in small samples
CausesOverfitting (slope < 1), shrinkage (slope > 1), resampling or class weights (intercept), model family, dataset shift
RecalibrationPlatt / temperature (logistic), isotonic (monotone steps, needs data), fit on separate data
Base-rate shiftAdd log[(π′/(1 − π′))/(π/(1 − π))] to the logit when only the prior moved
Log-loss vs BrierLog-loss punishes confident errors without limit; Brier is bounded; AUC cannot see calibration
SegmentsAverage calibration can hide opposite errors; check by segment
Decisionst* = c_FP/(c_FP + c_FN) needs calibrated q; NB(t) = TP/n − FP/n · t/(1 − t)
TestsHL, Spiegelhalter, binomial by grade: reject too often at large n, too rarely at small n; report slope with CI

item 17 Imbalanced data and rare events

IdeaOne line
Accuracy paradoxPredicting “no event” is 99.5% accurate at 0.5% prevalence; use recall, precision, PR curves, MCC
ROC vs PRROC-AUC is prevalence-free; precision falls with prevalence; PR baseline = prevalence
PPVsens·π / [sens·π + (1 − spec)(1 − π)]; tiny π means most alerts are false
Operating pointF1 (threshold ≈ F*/2 for calibrated p), cost t* = c_FP/(c_FP + c_FN), or capacity top-m
InformationComes from events; ≥ 10 events per parameter is a minimum, check Riley-type criteria
ResamplingNo new information; shifts probabilities; do only inside training folds
Prior correctionUndersample non-events with fraction r: add ln r to the intercept; slope variance × ≈ (1 + π/r)
Rare-event biasML under-predicts event probability, can separate; use Firth, ridge or Bayesian priors
Cost-sensitiveWeight w on events ≡ threshold 1/(1 + w) for a correct model; weighting costs variance and calibration
EvaluationStratify, repeat, report intervals; out-of-time test; don’t tune the threshold on the test set
Rare ratesWald fails; Clopper–Pearson (conservative), Wilson, Jeffreys; 0 events → upper bound ≈ 3/n

item 19 Missing data

IdeaOne line
MCAR / MAR / MNARMissingness independent of data / depends on observed data / depends on the missing value itself; an assumption, not testable from the data alone
Complete casesUnbiased for regression if missingness depends on covariates, not the outcome; biased if it depends on the outcome; always loses precision
Single imputationMean fill shrinks variance and correlation; regression fill inflates correlation; even stochastic fill gives SEs that are too small
Multiple imputationDraw M completions from the posterior predictive (parameters too), analyse each, pool with Rubin: T = W̄ + (1 + 1/M)B
Fraction missing infoγ = (1 + 1/M)B/T; efficiency (1 + γ/M)⁻¹; use M of at least the percentage of incomplete cases
Imputation modelInclude the outcome, all analysis variables (and interactions) and predictors of missingness; chained equations for many incomplete variables; check convergence
Skewed variablesTransform or use predictive mean matching, which imputes real observed donor values
MNARNot identifiable; do delta-adjusted or reference-based sensitivity analysis and report the tipping point
WeightingIPW = 1/P(observed | x), needs positivity; AIPW is doubly robust (either the outcome or the selection model right)
Reject inferenceOutcome missing for rejected applicants; MAR if the policy used recorded variables; manual overrides make it MNAR
Prediction modelsImputation rule fixed on training data; indicator helps if missingness is informative but is fragile under deployment shift; monitor missing rates
ReportingAmount and patterns, mechanism argument, imputation model, M and seed, pooled results with γ, sensitivity analysis

item 20 Dependent data: time series, panels, mixed models

item 21 Multiple testing and selective inference

IdeaOne line
MultiplicityP(≥ 1 false positive) = 1 − (1 − α)^m; the family includes hidden choices (subgroups, outcomes, cut-offs, models)
FWER vs FDRFWER = P(V ≥ 1), for confirmatory claims. FDR = E[V/R], for screening; neither is controlled by testing each at α
Bonferroni / Holmα/m; Holm is step-down with α/(m − k + 1) and dominates Bonferroni under any dependence
Benjamini–HochbergLargest k with p₍ₖ₎ ≤ kq/m; FDR = π₀q under independence or PRDS; BY divides q by Σ1/i for arbitrary dependence
Storey / q-valueπ̂₀ = #{p > λ}/((1 − λ)m); BH at q/π̂₀; more power when many effects are real
FDR is an averageThe FDP of one list varies, more so with correlated tests
Winner’s curseSelected estimates are biased up; confirm on new data, shrink (empirical Bayes), use hierarchical models
SubgroupsTest the interaction once; “significant here, not there” is not evidence of heterogeneity; pre-specify and adjust
Selective inferenceNaive intervals fail after selection; use simultaneous, conditional (truncated), split or replication intervals
Backtest overfittingBest of K noise strategies has Sharpe ≈ SE·√(2 ln K) in sample and 0 out of sample; deflate by the number of trials
Local fdr, empirical nullfdr(z) = π₀f₀/f; estimate the null from the bulk when the theoretical N(0,1) is too narrow

item 22 Confounding, selection bias, colliders, Simpson’s paradox

IdeaOne line

item 26 Bayesian inference

IdeaOne line

item 27 MCMC and variational inference

IdeaOne line
Why samplePosterior summaries are integrals; Monte Carlo error is 1/√M in any dimension, whereas a grid needs 100^d points
Importance samplingWeights w = p/q; ESS = (Σw)²/Σw²; the proposal needs tails at least as heavy as the target, or the weights have infinite variance
Metropolis–HastingsAccept with min(1, p(θ*)/p(θ)): only a ratio, so the normalising constant cancels; step size tuned for about 44% (1-d) or 23% (many-d) acceptance
GibbsDraw each parameter from its full conditional; no tuning, always accepted; slow when parameters are highly correlated (ESS fraction (1 − ρ²)/(1 + ρ²) for a bivariate normal)
Warm-up and chainsSeveral chains from dispersed starts; discard warm-up; fix and record the seed
R̂Between- over within-chain variance on split chains; require below 1.01; blind to modes no chain visits
ESS and MCSEESS = n/(1 + 2Σρ_k); MCSE = sd/√ESS; do not thin to gain precision; check bulk-ESS and tail-ESS
HMC and NUTSGradient-guided long moves with a Metropolis correction; ε near the narrowest width; NUTS picks the trajectory length; divergences signal biased exploration
Logistic regressionStandardise predictors; use the Laplace covariance to shape proposals; correlated predictors give correlated coefficients
Laplace approximationNormal at the mode with the inverse Hessian; exact for normal posteriors; poor for skewed, bounded or multimodal ones
Variational inferenceMaximise the ELBO (minimise KL(q ‖ p)) over a simple family; fast; mean-field underestimates variance and has thin tails
Tail probabilitiesRelative error ≈ √((1 − p)/(p · ESS)): rare events need many effective draws
WorkflowTest on simulated data, several chains, R̂ and ESS and divergences, MCSE below the posterior sd, predictive checks, prior sensitivity
